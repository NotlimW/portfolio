More of it, without lowering the bar

I keep close to what's actually possible right now, not what's hyped — new models, new tools, tested as they land. Used well, that means a version that used to be too expensive to produce, or too slow to justify, is suddenly worth making. I generate the assets myself — images, video effects, voiceovers, graphics — then direct, edit, and lay them out by hand, so the same material keeps working across the next iteration, or an entirely different format.

Half the week was never the creative half.

AI for systems Workflows Automations Apps

The boring half of the week, automated

The research comes first — sitting with the actual work until the real bottleneck shows itself, not the one that's easiest to blame. From there it's a plan: a workflow stitched together from existing tools, an automation that runs on its own, or, when neither fits, a small app built specifically for that one repetitive job. None of it is the interesting part of the work — which is exactly why it's worth removing. What's left is more time on the half that actually moves things forward.

The read I keep coming back to, after trying most of what comes out: AI is the fastest way I've found to reach a good decision — never a shortcut around making one.

How I work

01

Understand

Interviews, data, competitors, and a lot of questions. I want the real problem, not the stated one — they are rarely the same sentence.

02

Explore

Wide and fast. Directions on the table before anything gets precious, so we're choosing between options rather than defending the first one.

03

Build

Figma libraries, components, code. Made to be handed over and lived with, which usually means less clever and more maintainable.

04

Prove

Ship, measure, adjust. Design that performs is the only kind worth defending in a room full of people with different opinions.
